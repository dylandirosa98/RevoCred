    PROPOSAL ONLY · NOT IMPLEMENTED IN THE APP

    Choose a fixed strategy using the new questionnaire

    Keep the nine original card lists. Replace the hidden fee branch with explicit simplicity and experience signals. These are proposed product rules—not facts inferred from spending, income, fee approval, or likelihood of credit approval.

    Important trade-off: the new questions cannot reveal willingness to pay annual fees. Premium paths below are candidates to review, not permission to apply. The proposal does not add an onboarding question, but would require a fee-and-terms acknowledgement before any application action. That gate is not built here.

      Your exact decision path

        Proposed rules, in order
        1. Credit first: building → S01 cash back / S02 travel; fair → S03 cash back / S04 travel. A large wallet target never overrides the starting credit band.
        2. Good/excellent + cash back: 1–2 cards → S03; 2–4 or 4+ → S07. Both remain the original two-card plans; wanting more cards does not mean we must fill that quota.
        3. Good/excellent + travel, no travel focus: provisional S04 foundation only; flag conflicting answers. Do not reinterpret “none” as “both” or promote to premium.
        4. Broader exploration: 4+ cards and established/optimizing life stage allows a premium candidate. This is a conservative complexity threshold, not fee consent.
        5. Domestic travel: broader exploration → S08; otherwise S04.
        6. International travel: broader exploration → S05; otherwise S06. This keeps the original international targets rather than treating card count as an arbitrary issuer preference.
        7. Domestic + international: broader exploration + excellent → S09; broader exploration + good → S06; otherwise S04.
        8. After selection: preserve every original card name/order. Show already-owned cards; flag three-card plans against a 1–2-card preference. Do not secretly drop a matrix card, force a fourth card, or close existing accounts.

        Unavoidable gaps—not hidden assumptions

        • Several original travel strategies contain three cards. No exact one- or two-card travel template exists; a strict 1–2-card limit cannot be fully met without changing the matrix.
        • S01 names Savor for Students; the adult-life-stage question does not confirm student eligibility. Keep a visible eligibility hold rather than substituting a different product silently.
        • A 4+ preference does not prove willingness to pay fees. Premium results remain review candidates until the member accepts current terms.
        • Choosing 2–4 vs 4+ is a proposal for complexity—not a mathematically equivalent replacement for the original fee question. Javed can review every changed case in the proposed table.
